The problem

Cleaning follows the clock. Usage does not.

A restroom next to a packed lecture hall and one in an empty hallway get the same schedule. The difference only shows up when someone complains.

01

The schedule does not know about breaks

Fixed schedules ignore shift changes, events in the amphitheater and exam days.

02

Whoever notices is whoever complains

Running out of paper or soap is usually discovered by a student, not by the team.

03

No records, no improvement

Without history, nobody knows how long it took, who handled it or what was restocked.

System goal
When usage reaches the configured level, the team gets an alert to inspect, clean and restock, and everything is logged.

User journeys

Built for those who clean, those who manage and those who use it.

Journey 1 · System

The whole cycle, without anyone having to remember.

From the first sensor event to the closing record, every step follows a rule and is documented.

  1. Sensor logs the entries
  2. Level 1: restroom near its threshold
  3. Level 2: ticket opened and team identified
  4. Level 3: escalated if still pending
  5. Team receives it and runs the checklist
  6. Supplies and completion are logged
Journey 3 · Cleaning team

Knows where to go and what to do there.

Gets the ticket on the phone, finds the restroom on the 3D map and follows the checklist. Logs what was restocked and what was missing.

  1. Gets the ticket notification
  2. Checks the location on the 3D map
  3. Takes the ticket
  4. Runs the digital checklist
  5. Logs restocking and incidents
  6. Closes the job
Journey 4 · Management

The whole campus on one screen.

Tickets by status and priority, available teams, supply usage and response time. Reports by period, building, restroom and team.

  1. Opens the main dashboard
  2. Sees restrooms and pending tickets
  3. Tracks teams, priorities and indicators
  4. Reviews productivity, usage and timing
  5. Adjusts operations and tracks the results
Journey 2 · Requester

Spotted a problem? It takes seconds.

Pick the building and restroom, describe the issue and you are done. The ticket goes to the right team, regardless of the sensor count.

  1. Opens UniSense
  2. Selects the building and restroom
  3. Describes the issue
  4. The system logs it and opens a ticket
  5. The responsible team receives it
  6. Follows the progress

FAQ

What people usually ask.

Does the sensor count people inside the restroom, or entries?

Entries. The goal is to know how many times the restroom was used since the last cleaning, not how many people are inside right now.

Are the 20, 30 and 45 thresholds fixed?

No. The administrator tunes each restroom individually to its real usage, and the opening and escalation rules are documented.

When does the count go back to zero?

Only after the team completes the checklist and the work is approved. Opening a ticket resets nothing.

What if a sensor stops responding?

The dashboard flags the restroom as offline and logs the unmonitored period. All other restrooms keep working.

Do we need hardware to get started?

No. Development starts with event simulation to validate the logic. Physical sensors come later, through standard interfaces.

How is personal data handled?

Securely hashed passwords, HTTPS, role-based access and an audit trail. Data handling follows Brazil's LGPD privacy law.
